The liability and the hedges

The client owes a single payment in 10 years of $100M × CPI growth, starting 31 Dec 2017, valued every month at the long RRB real yield. Each hedge starts at the liability's value and is fully funded. Proxy weights were set on 2004–2017 data only, so everything shown from 2018 is out of sample.

Can CPI be forecast?

Each model was re-estimated every month from Dec 2017 using only the data known at that time, then scored on the inflation that followed. Monthly CPI changes have no AR persistence once seasonality is removed, so the AR models forecast 12-month inflation directly.

Method, data and caveats

Method
  • Liability: $100M × CPIT/CPI0 paid 10 years after the start, discounted at the Bank of Canada long RRB real yield (semi-annual). CPI enters with its publication lag: at each month-end only the previous month's CPI is known.
  • RRB benchmark: each month, the two RRBs whose durations bracket the liability's duration, weighted to match its value and duration. Variants hold the longest RRB plus T-bills, or the longest and shortest RRBs.
  • Proxy hedge: weights minimise the volatility of monthly hedge-minus-liability returns on 2004–2017 data for a constant 10-year liability. Out of sample, rate positions are scaled by the liability's duration ÷ 10 and the rest is held in T-bills.
  • Index ratio: RRB reference CPI is the CPI three months back, interpolated through the month.
  • Breakeven: nominal GoC yield at each RRB's maturity (interpolated from the 2, 5, 10 and 30-year benchmarks) minus the RRB's real yield.
  • Forecasts: direct AR regressions of next-h-month inflation on 12-month and seasonally adjusted 3-month inflation (plus oil); a VAR(1) of CPI inflation, four yields and six asset returns with month dummies (lag order chosen by AIC, BIC and HQ).
  • Monte Carlo: the same VAR(1), with shocks drawn from its own historical residuals and long-run levels anchored to today's yields and the 2.25% breakeven. Weights are chosen on one set of 4,000 ten-year paths and judged on a fresh set. Tail-optimised weights maximise the average of the worst 5% outcomes, with tracking error capped at 3% a year and no leverage.
Data
  • Statistics Canada tables 18-10-0004-01 (CPI, monthly, not seasonally adjusted) and 18-10-0007-01 (basket weights).
  • Bank of Canada selected bond yields (2, 5, 10-year, long nominal and long RRB real yield).
  • Bloomberg: month-end prices for the eight remaining RRBs (from Oct 2015; 2050 from 2017; 2054 from 2021), BCOM total return sub-indices in CAD, US TIPS total return index, Canada 3-month T-bill.
  • S&P Capital IQ: S&P/TSX Capped Real Estate and Energy total return.
  • FRED: US CPI, 10-year TIPS yield and breakeven, USD/CAD, WTI, US 3-month T-bill, IMF food price index.
  • Vendor data are not redistributed here; this page shows only derived results. Data downloaded 20 Nov 2026; monthly analysis to 31 Oct 2026.
Caveats
  • RRB prices before Oct 2015 (used only in the start-date test) are modelled from the nominal curve and a flat breakeven; checked against Bloomberg on the overlap (monthly price-change correlation 0.83–0.99).
  • The commodity sleeve's smaller end gap is largely the return premium of commodities and equities over cash, not better tracking of CPI: it raises monthly tracking error and lost up to 3.2 points of the liability for starts before the 2015 oil collapse.
  • The hedgeable share counts basket weight only. Even gasoline, the best case, is only about half explained month to month out of sample.
  • 12-month correlations use overlapping windows, so they rest on roughly 20 independent years.
  • Breakeven is not pure expected inflation: the RRB liquidity premium pushes it down and any inflation risk premium pushes it up. CAD inflation swap quotes would measure these.
  • The VAR does not generate 2021-style surges on its own, which is why the stress tests are shown. In the simulation, the RRB hedge's tracking noise is drawn from its 2004–2026 history.
